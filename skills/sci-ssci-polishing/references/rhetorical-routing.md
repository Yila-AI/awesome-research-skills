# Rhetorical routing

Route by the paragraph's job, not merely by its heading. Interdisciplinary papers may mix SCI and SSCI conventions.

## Abstract

Typical order:

```text
problem/context -> gap/objective -> approach -> principal findings -> calibrated implication
```

- Make the research object and contribution explicit early.
- Report central evidence before broad implications.
- Keep methods compact but concrete enough to identify design and data.
- Do not introduce claims, citations, or limitations absent from the source.
- Avoid promotional adjectives such as “groundbreaking,” “novel,” or “remarkable” unless they are necessary technical claims and supported.

## Introduction

Typical progression:

```text
established importance -> unresolved problem -> limits of existing work -> present study -> contribution
```

- Prefer gap statements that identify what remains unknown over generic claims that “few studies exist.”
- Separate field-level background from the current study's action.
- End with a precise study objective, research question, hypothesis, or contribution that matches the source.

SCI tendency: move quickly from the scientific problem to the mechanism, method, or testable objective.

SSCI tendency: synthesize competing explanations, define constructs, motivate theoretical relationships, and show how the study changes an ongoing conversation.

## Literature review / theory

- Organize by ideas, mechanisms, debates, or boundary conditions rather than author-by-author summary.
- Use citations as evidence for propositions, not decoration.
- Distinguish consensus, disagreement, and missing evidence.
- Preserve whether a statement is empirical, theoretical, or interpretive.
- Do not fabricate a bridge between literatures when the source does not establish one.

## Methods

Primary objective: reproducibility.

- Preserve chronological or procedural order.
- Keep actor, action, material/data, parameter, and condition explicit.
- Prefer stable terminology over stylistic variation.
- Do not remove repetitions that are necessary to distinguish samples, studies, waves, models, or analytical stages.
- Maintain preregistration, ethics, exclusion, missing-data, and robustness details exactly.

SCI tendency: emphasize materials, conditions, measurements, algorithms, and analytic parameters.

SSCI tendency: emphasize sampling, setting, construct operationalization, procedure, identification strategy, coding, and analytical rationale.

## Results / findings

Typical move:

```text
analysis/question -> observed result -> quantitative or qualitative evidence -> limited interpretation
```

- Lead with the finding when the setup is already known.
- Keep estimates and uncertainty adjacent to the relevant comparison.
- Preserve null results and contradictory evidence.
- Do not import discussion-level mechanism or significance into a results-only passage.
- In qualitative work, distinguish participant evidence, analytic interpretation, and theoretical claim.

## Discussion

Typical progression:

```text
principal finding -> interpretation -> relation to prior work -> contribution/implication -> limitation -> next step
```

- Start from findings actually reported.
- Mark interpretation as interpretation.
- Use calibrated verbs and hedges that match design strength.
- Preserve alternative explanations and limitations.
- Avoid repeating the Results section sentence by sentence.

SCI tendency: explain mechanism, generalizability, comparison with prior evidence, and technical or clinical significance.

SSCI tendency: articulate theoretical contribution, revise relationships among constructs, identify boundary conditions, and connect evidence to institutional or social context.

## Conclusion

- Synthesize rather than re-list results.
- Match the scope of the data and design.
- Keep practical or policy implications conditional when the evidence is conditional.
- Do not end with inflated universal claims.

## Full-section coherence checks

Before rewriting a full section, create a private move map with one row per paragraph:

```text
paragraph -> primary rhetorical job -> protected claims/evidence -> citation bundle -> intended relation to next paragraph
```

Revise locally first, then change paragraph order only when the map reveals a genuine progression problem. Sentence length is a diagnostic signal, not a target; never split, merge, or compress a sentence if doing so changes citation scope or separates a qualification from its claim.

Then verify:

1. no two adjacent paragraphs perform the same job without a reason;
2. each transition names a real relation: continuation, contrast, cause, consequence, example, or narrowing;
3. pronouns and shorthand have unambiguous antecedents;
4. terminology stays stable across the section;
5. the final paragraph fulfills the section's rhetorical purpose.
