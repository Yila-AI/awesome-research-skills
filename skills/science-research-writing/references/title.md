# Title

## Reader expectation

Make a concise, searchable promise about the study that the paper can fully keep.

## Required functions

Select only elements needed for accuracy and retrieval:

- central topic, exposure/intervention, method, or outcome;
- population or setting when it defines scope;
- study design when it prevents causal or evidential misunderstanding;
- relationship type at the supported strength;
- article type when required by the venue.

## Target-paper observations

Observe length, punctuation, design labels, population naming, question versus declarative form, abbreviations, and subtitle use. Do not copy a target title structure when it distorts the present study.

## Section boundaries

Do not promise lasting effects, causation, mechanisms, external validation, universal scope, clinical utility, or comprehensiveness unless the paper supplies the necessary evidence.

## Failure modes

- causal verbs for observational designs;
- omitting a setting that materially limits generalization;
- naming an outcome not reported as primary evidence;
- using `novel`, `first`, or `comprehensive` without verification;
- optimizing keywords at the expense of accuracy;
- promising more than the Abstract and Results deliver.

## Final audit

Run the Title-Paper Promise Check: map every title element to the objective, design, population, variables, evidence, validation setting, and conclusion. Remove or qualify any unsupported promise.
