# Certainty and claim-strength contract

The supplied evidence and author intent control claim strength. Fluent prose is not permission to strengthen or weaken a proposition.

## Claim-strength ladder

```text
is consistent with / may suggest
< is associated with / relates to
< predicts
< contributes to
< affects / leads to
< causes / demonstrates that
```

Field conventions can alter the exact ordering, but a draft or revision must never move silently in either direction. A substantive move requires new evidence and explicit author authorization.

## Preserve these distinctions

- observed result vs author interpretation vs prior literature;
- association vs prediction vs explanation vs causation;
- statistical significance vs practical or clinical importance;
- absence of evidence vs evidence of no effect;
- exploratory vs confirmatory analysis;
- internal performance vs external validation;
- sample-specific observation vs population-level generalization;
- feasibility or acceptability vs effectiveness;
- short-term outcome vs lasting effect.

## Certainty markers

Treat modal verbs, hedges, negation, exceptions, and boundary phrases as semantic content. Preserve or query changes to terms such as `may`, `might`, `could`, `suggests`, `appears`, `is consistent with`, `did not`, `only`, `within this sample`, and `under these conditions`.

Do not add hedging mechanically to every sentence. Match the evidence, study design, analysis, and author-authorized interpretation.

## Null and adverse findings

- Preserve null, negative, and adverse findings even when they complicate the narrative.
- Do not relabel a non-significant result as a trend unless the author explicitly supports that interpretation.
- Do not omit a finding because it weakens the conclusion.
- Keep outcome, time point, comparison, and uncertainty together.

## Author authorization

When a stronger or weaker claim may be scientifically appropriate:

1. show the current evidence-faithful wording;
2. identify the proposed movement on the ladder;
3. state what additional evidence or author judgment would justify it;
4. obtain explicit authorization before changing the manuscript.
