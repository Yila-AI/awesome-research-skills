# Introduction

## Reader expectation

Help the reader understand what is known, what remains unresolved, why that gap matters, and exactly what the present study does.

## Required functions

1. Establish the specific research territory and its importance.
2. Synthesize the knowledge needed to understand the problem.
3. Narrow to a consequential unresolved issue.
4. Define the gap without claiming that no research exists unless verified.
5. State the present study's objective, question, or hypothesis using the supplied design and scope.

Build a funnel only when the target field uses one. Keep every cited proposition attached to its supplied citation.

## Target-paper observations

Observe where the target papers state importance, how quickly they narrow, how they synthesize rather than list prior work, where they place the gap, and how explicitly they preview design or hypotheses. Model recurring functions and alternatives, not wording.

## Section boundaries

Do not report unsupplied results, interpret findings, introduce methods detail needed only for replication, or manufacture citations. A brief approach preview is acceptable only when supported and conventional for the target venue.

## Failure modes

- starting with a universal claim too broad for the evidence;
- literature-by-literature summary without synthesis;
- gap inflation such as `no studies have examined` without verification;
- a study aim that does not answer the stated gap;
- adding hypotheses because the section appears incomplete;
- changing a user-supplied exploratory question into a confirmatory prediction.

## Final audit

Confirm that the gap follows from the supplied literature, the objective matches the design, citations remain attached to their propositions, and the final paragraph promises only what the study actually evaluates.
