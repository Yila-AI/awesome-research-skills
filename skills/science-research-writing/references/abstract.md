# Abstract

## Reader expectation

Provide an accurate, self-contained decision aid: why the study was done, what was done, what was found, and what the evidence supports.

## Required functions

Follow the journal's structured or unstructured format while preserving:

1. focused background or problem;
2. objective or question;
3. design, setting, sample, core methods, and analysis needed for interpretation;
4. primary result with magnitude and uncertainty when supplied;
5. important null, negative, or adverse result when material;
6. evidence-calibrated conclusion and boundary.

## Target-paper observations

Observe heading structure, word allocation, numeric density, placement of design and sample, and conclusion strength. Use target papers to prioritize functions, not to supply content.

## Section boundaries

Do not add citations unless required and supplied, introduce results absent from the paper, omit an outcome that changes interpretation, or make the Abstract stronger than Results and Discussion.

## Failure modes

- vague results without numbers when numbers were supplied;
- background consuming space needed for evidence;
- conclusion claiming causality from association;
- omitting sample or setting while generalizing broadly;
- hiding null or adverse outcomes;
- violating the requested word limit by adding generic context.

## Final audit

Cross-check every Abstract fact against the manuscript materials. Verify objective-design-result-conclusion alignment, all numeric tokens, sample and setting, result direction, limitations, and word limit.
