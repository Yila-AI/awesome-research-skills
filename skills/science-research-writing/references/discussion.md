# Discussion

## Reader expectation

Explain what the findings mean, how they relate to the research question and supplied literature, what can and cannot be inferred, and why the bounded contribution matters.

## Required functions

Use the sequence that best fits the evidence:

1. state the principal finding without stronger language than Results;
2. connect the finding to the research question;
3. compare with supplied prior evidence;
4. offer only author-supported interpretations or mechanisms;
5. distinguish implication from recommendation;
6. state limitations and boundary conditions;
7. identify a defensible next research step;
8. close with a calibrated contribution.

## Target-paper observations

Observe how target papers alternate finding, comparison, interpretation, implication, and limitation; how early limitations appear; and how conclusions are calibrated. Do not reuse their mechanisms, citations, or claims.

## Section boundaries

Do not introduce new results, fabricate literature, infer causality from observational evidence, generalize beyond the sample or setting, or add a limitation merely because it is conventional. Ask the author when interpretation is missing.

A design label authorizes only its direct inference boundary. Do not invent specific reverse-causality accounts, confounders, mechanisms, alternative explanations, future study designs, or practical recommendations. Do not reinterpret a null result beyond the outcome, comparison, and analysis the author supplied.

Do not infer that one variable is more relevant, more important, or meaningfully different because its association is significant while another association is not. Such a contrast requires a supplied direct comparison. Avoid relabeling an exposure as a `factor`, `determinant`, or intervention target when only an association is available.

When no direct comparison is supplied, report each result separately. Avoid synthesis such as `but not`, `whereas`, `not all indicators`, `different pattern`, or `more relevant` when it rhetorically implies that the two associations differ.

## Failure modes

- restating Results without interpretation;
- treating association as causation;
- deleting null or adverse findings from the narrative;
- adding a plausible but untested mechanism;
- converting an implication into immediate policy or clinical adoption;
- placing limitations in a token final sentence with no effect on claims.
- using generic methodological knowledge to manufacture a fuller Discussion;
- offering future longitudinal, experimental, clinical, or policy work not requested or supplied.

## Final audit

Trace each interpretation to a reported result or explicit author judgment. Check comparison direction, citation attachment, claim-strength level, limitation-conclusion alignment, external-validity boundaries, and visibility of null findings.
