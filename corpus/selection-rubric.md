# Corpus selection method

The corpus was built as a staged portfolio rather than a simple citation ranking. Venue reputation and citation signals were used for initial prioritization, but no paper was selected only because it was highly cited or published in a prestigious journal.

## Stage 1: 1,000-paper metadata pool

- Balance SCI and SSCI at 500 papers each.
- Cover 50 established journals across nine broad discipline clusters.
- Sample 20 records per journal: 12 established/high-citation candidates and 8 recent candidates.
- Exclude obvious editorials, corrections, retractions, protocols, commentaries, calls for papers, and similar non-target article types.

The pool is a screening universe. Inclusion does not mean that all 1,000 full texts were downloaded, read, or distilled.

## Stage 2: 200-paper shortlist

Retain four candidates per journal using a composite screening score based on:

- venue-quality evidence;
- publication recency;
- normalized citation signals;
- metadata completeness;
- article-type validity;
- usefulness for the target research-design portfolio.

Citation counts and venue indicators are heuristics, not article-quality verdicts.

## Stage 3: balanced 60-paper portfolio

Select 60 papers while enforcing:

- SCI 30; SSCI 30;
- coverage of all nine discipline clusters;
- diversity of empirical, experimental, computational, survey, qualitative, observational, and conceptual designs;
- no more than three papers from one journal in the final portfolio;
- final peer-reviewed versions rather than unverified preprints;
- lawful full-text availability for material used in distillation or calibration.

## Frozen roles

- 40 papers: distillation;
- 10 papers: calibration;
- 10 papers: sealed blind evaluation.

Blind papers remained metadata-only until the Skill was fixed and hashed. After the freeze, nine verified blind full texts were available for scoring; the inaccessible tenth paper was excluded rather than replaced post hoc.

## Paper-level review dimensions

| Dimension | Question |
|---|---|
| Venue evidence | Is there reproducible evidence that the venue is established in its field? |
| Article validity | Is this a final peer-reviewed research article of the intended type? |
| Writing utility | Does it expose clear section and paragraph functions that can support abstraction? |
| Design coverage | Does it add a needed study design rather than duplicate the portfolio? |
| Field coverage | Does it improve balance across the nine discipline clusters? |
| Access status | Can the full text be accessed lawfully for its assigned role? |
| Metadata quality | Are title, DOI, venue, year, article type, and provenance sufficiently complete? |
| Contamination control | Was its distillation, calibration, or blind role fixed before analysis? |

## Hard exclusions

- Preprints without a verified final journal version.
- Editorials, letters, corrections, book reviews, news, protocols, and conference abstracts.
- Papers whose assigned full-text use could not be lawfully supported.
- Duplicate articles, duplicate cohorts, or excessive concentration in one journal or author group.
- Articles selected only for prestige but with little reusable writing structure.
